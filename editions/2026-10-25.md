## 1. The work the economy forgot to count

*India · Work, gender & public data*

India’s statistics ministry has proposed changes to the Periodic Labour Force Survey from January. A new category would identify digital-platform workers; fuller questions would ask about cooking, cleaning and caring for children or older people, including whether household responsibilities keep someone from seeking paid work.

India’s 2024 time-use survey found females over six spent more than four and a half hours a day on unpaid domestic and care work, compared with 37 minutes for males. Naming that labour can improve statistics and expose constraint. It does not itself create wages, rest or social protection.

Scripture’s economic vision repeatedly notices work and vulnerability that powerful ledgers miss: gleaners, widows, servants, strangers. Better counting is a modest act of truth. What is owed to the person whose care makes everybody else’s paid day possible?

**A question to carry**

What changes after invisible work becomes statistically visible?

## 2. A funeral became a target

*Jordan · Violence, grief & coexistence*

Three masked gunmen opened fire on Friday at Saint George Greek Orthodox Church in Hamoud, Jordan. By Saturday, nine people had died and nearly 40 were wounded. No group had claimed responsibility; officials said it was too early to establish links to any organisation.

A funeral is where a community gathers unarmed, already carrying grief. The attack also struck at the trust that allows a minority community to mourn in public. Karak has been regarded as a place of religious coexistence; one survivor said that security had vanished.

Anger will demand a name before evidence can supply one. Restraint in assigning guilt protects neighbours from becoming substitutes for unknown perpetrators. Lament can tell the truth without recruiting the dead into wider suspicion.

**A question to carry**

How can a society pursue the attackers without letting grief be conscripted into distrust of an entire community?

## 3. Libya draws one maritime line with two governments

*Libya, Turkey & eastern Mediterranean · Diplomacy, borders & energy*

Libya’s eastern-based parliament approved the 2019 Turkey–Libya maritime agreement on Tuesday, reversing its former opposition. The Tripoli government welcomed the vote. Greece, Cyprus and Egypt reject the deal, drawn across waters where offshore gas has intensified competing claims.

The vote is a curious kind of unity: rival Libyan camps are moving closer around an external claim before resolving their internal division. Supporters say it protects Libya’s economic interests. Greece says it disregards the rights of islands between Turkish and Libyan coasts; the European Union says it violates third-country rights.

Lines at sea look clean because maps omit the bargaining. Here a border is law, energy prospect, diplomatic alliance and proof that a fractured state can still speak with one voice—when the possible prize lies under water.

**A question to carry**

Can an agreement ease a country’s internal division while exporting the dispute to its neighbours?

## 4. Nature’s targets reach their audit

*Global · Yerevan, Armenia · Nature, finance & public policy*

Governments meeting in Yerevan are conducting the first global review of the 2022 Kunming–Montreal biodiversity framework. A draft UN assessment says the world is off track on 22 of 23 targets. Negotiators face a $30 billion public-finance goal and roughly $500 billion a year in subsidies for activities that damage nature.

Governments can pay to restore a wetland while subsidising the farming, fishing or extraction that degrades it. Conservation becomes a mop beneath a running tap. Reform is difficult because every harmful incentive is also somebody’s income or promise to voters.

Creation is neither scenery nor a luxury for easier decades. Food, water and economic stability live inside ecosystems, however neatly budgets file them elsewhere. A target becomes credible when government examines the money flowing against it.

**A question to carry**

Which subsidy would reveal whether the promise to protect nature is serious?

## 5. The fire is reaching backward into human memory

*Indonesia · Climate, archaeology & human memory*

Researchers say heat and smoke from Indonesia’s worst wildfires since 2015 could accelerate damage to cave paintings in South Sulawesi that are more than 67,000 years old. A severe El Niño has raised temperatures and reduced rainfall, leaving forests and peatlands unusually vulnerable to fire.

The paintings carry a numinous hush because they collapse our vanity about being modern. Long before our nations, museums and arguments over culture, human beings were already making images on stone. The fires threaten habitat and health in the present; they may also erase evidence of the imagination that preceded us.

Heritage protection usually sounds like care for the past. Here it requires climate and fire policy in the present. Memory does not survive by admiration alone; it needs cool rock, stable air and living forests outside the cave.

**A question to carry**

What do we owe an artwork made by people whose names, language and world have disappeared?

## 6. The camera kept rolling; the reporter moved

*Australia · Floods, courage & ordinary life*

ABC journalist Will Murray was reporting on flooding north of Brisbane on Thursday when a four-wheel-drive stalled and began sinking. Murray and bystander Daniel Hainey swam out and pulled the driver free moments before the vehicle submerged. More than 200 millimetres of rain had brought flash floods and hundreds of distress calls.

The rescue deserves admiration without becoming an invitation to imitate danger casually. Floodwater is powerful, vehicles can vanish quickly, and emergency advice remains plain: do not drive into it. Yet in the moment, two people kept their heads while a stranger’s life was narrowing to seconds.

The Good Samaritan in Luke 10 is remembered for compassion that crossed a social boundary. Its sharper edge is interruption: the traveller allowed another person’s emergency to overrule his itinerary. A news assignment became a rescue because the person in the water mattered more than the role behind the camera.

**A question to carry**

What helps courage remain clear-eyed rather than merely dramatic?

## 7. Mumbai borrows another bat

*India · Sport, culture & discovery*

Former Major League Baseball players and softball stars ran drills at Hansraj Morarji School in Mumbai this week. Visitors said the children’s cricket skills transferred readily to baseball; they were especially impressed by the girls. On Saturday, Indian cricketers then joined an MLBx three-on-three event at the Mumbai Football Arena.

Sports organisations see a market, naturally; enthusiasm and expansion often arrive holding the same glove. Yet the clinic revealed something commerce does not own: skills migrate. A child learns timing, courage, coordination and joy in one game, then discovers those capacities have another accent.

Cricket’s kingdom made room for a cousin. The charming detail is that the children did not have to become beginners in every sense. Human formation is often like that: what patience learns in one field quietly becomes courage in another.

**A question to carry**

Which abilities in us are waiting for a new game in which to become visible?
