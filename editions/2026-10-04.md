## 1. A missing name is not a clerical detail

*India · Democracy and citizenship*

Police detained several people in New Delhi on 3 October during a second day of protests against changes to India’s voter rolls. Critics of the nationwide verification exercise allege that millions of eligible voters have been removed; the Election Commission says the work is necessary to remove duplicate, deceased or ineligible entries. On Friday, it instructed officials to register eligible voters who had been omitted.

The strongest case for verification is obvious: a roll containing invalid names weakens trust. The strongest case against careless verification is equally obvious: a legitimate citizen may lose a vote. Electoral integrity and inclusion are not competing luxuries.

A lacuna is a missing part in a text. On an electoral roll, the blank may be a person. A democracy should make deletion reasoned, visible, appealable and swiftly repairable—not merely administratively tidy.

Can a process call itself clean if an ordinary citizen cannot easily discover why a name disappeared?

## 2. The runway is part of the journey

*Portugal · United States · Iran · Law, alliances and war*

Portuguese prosecutors opened an inquiry into the government’s authorisation of US flights through Lajes Air Base in the Azores during the Iran conflict. A bilateral agreement permits American use of the base without prior approval in peacetime, but Portuguese authorisation is required once hostilities begin. The inquiry follows an opposition complaint and is not a finding of illegality.

Foreign Minister Paulo Rangel says Portugal acted lawfully after assurances that the operations would be defensive, necessary, proportionate and limited to military targets. That is the government’s strongest case. The harder truth is that modern war travels through permissions, fuel, airfields and legal memoranda before it travels through the sky.

Allies are expected to sing from the same hymn sheet. Law exists partly to ask whether harmony has become moral outsourcing. Portugal did not become irrelevant merely because another country owned the aircraft.

At what point does logistical support become moral and legal participation?

## 3. The harvest can be large while bread becomes dear

*Global · Food, trade and ordinary life*

The UN Food and Agriculture Organization’s food-price index rose 1.5% in September to 136.0 points, its highest level since November 2022. Sugar prices jumped 6.1% in a month and cereals 5.1%. Yet FAO still expects the world’s second-largest cereal harvest on record.

That apparent contradiction explains modern hunger better than a simple story of scarcity. Food must be grown, certainly, but it must also be financed, insured, shipped and delivered. Disruption in the Black Sea and Strait of Hormuz, together with severe weather, can make a plentiful harvest expensive before it reaches a family. Markets are magnificent at moving signals. Unfortunately, households cannot serve price signals for dinner.

Jesus teaches his disciples to pray for daily bread in Matthew 6:11. The request is humble, bodily and plural: give us. Bread comes as gift, yet Scripture never permits those who control its gates to forget the neighbour waiting outside them.

## 4. We recovered the years, but not all their health

*Global health · Health and human progress*

WHO’s latest estimates put global life expectancy at 73.3 years in 2023, almost back to the 73.4 years recorded before the pandemic in 2019. That is substantial recovery. Healthy life expectancy, however, reached 62.8 years—still 0.4 years below its earlier level. Noncommunicable diseases now account for 74% of global deaths, up from 58% in 2000.

The headline deserves celebration without triumphalism. Public health helped the world recover years that COVID-19 had stripped away. But longevity and wellbeing are not synonyms. As infections recede, dementia, diabetes, heart disease, depression and other long conditions occupy more of life’s interior.

A civilisation can become excellent at postponing death while remaining clumsy at accompanying frailty. Progress must count years, but it must also ask what care, dignity, relationship and relief inhabit them.

What do we mean by progress when people live longer but carry illness longer too?

## 5. The map has begun drawing itself again

*Indonesia · Earth science and wonder*

Indonesia’s Geology Agency says Anak Krakatau expanded by 32.8 hectares after twenty-five hours of eruptions from 4–6 September sent lava into the sea. Later satellite images detected two new formations, one northwest and another southeast of the volcano. Scientists need newer data before deciding whether they are new eruption centres or another kind of volcanic deposit.

Maps look permanent because human beings do not live long enough to watch continents think. Krakatau is less obliging. Here geography remains a verb: lava cools, coastlines move and the atlas quietly becomes outdated.

Wonder must not erase consequence. Ash closed airports and nearly 3,000 flights were cancelled, delayed or diverted, affecting more than 340,000 travellers. Still, there is humility in seeing new ground where yesterday there was water. The world is not a finished stage built solely for our entrance; it is an active creation whose timescale exceeds our confidence.

## 6. At ninety, the music was still allowed to change its mind

*United States · Scotland · Culture, age and creativity*

Composer Steve Reich turned ninety on Saturday. His latest work, In All Your Ways, premiered at the Edinburgh International Festival in August. More than halfway through writing it, Reich decided the music was moving too quickly. He inserted slow movements into an almost-finished piece—something his usual beginning-to-end method had never required.

At ninety, he remains willing to perform surgery on his own habits. Some of us become doctrinally committed to a first draft before the kettle has boiled. Reich listened until the work told him it could not breathe.

The title comes from Proverbs 3:6. Reich uses its opening words as musical possibility; the proverb itself is more definite: know God in all your ways, and he will make your paths straight. It does not baptise instinct. It subjects the whole path to God.

Concinnity means the elegant fitting of parts into a whole. Here it came not from preserving the plan but from humbly revising it.

What have I mistaken for faithfulness merely because I have always done it that way?

## A little language to take away

- **Lacuna** (luh-KYOO-nuh): A blank space, missing part or gap in something expected to be complete. *The audit revealed a serious lacuna in the record of who had been consulted.* [Dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/lacuna)
- **Concinnity** (kuhn-SIN-uh-tee): Harmony or elegance created when different parts fit skilfully into a whole. *The building's concinnity came from stone, light and silence working together.* [Dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/concinnity)
- **Sing from the same hymn sheet**: To express the same position publicly and appear to agree, especially within an organisation. *The ministers sang from the same hymn sheet in public, though their private accounts differed.* [Source](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/sing-from-the-same-hymn-sheet)
